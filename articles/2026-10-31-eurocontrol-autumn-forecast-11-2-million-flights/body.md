EUROCONTROL has cut its forecast for European air traffic this year. In its [Autumn 2026 Seven-Year Forecast](https://www.eurocontrol.int/publication/eurocontrol-forecast-2026-2032), published on 20 October and reported again this week by the Anadolu Agency (via [UA.NEWS](https://ua.news/en/money/eurocontrol-zniziv-prognoz-aviatrafiku-evropi-na-110-tisiach-reisiv-u-2026-rotsi-anadolu-agency)), the agency now expects **11.2 million instrument flight rules (IFR) flights** in the area of the European Civil Aviation Conference (ECAC) in 2026, growth of **1.7%** instead of the 2.7% it forecast in the spring.

## About 110,000 flights fewer

The main reason, according to EUROCONTROL, is the crisis in the Middle East. The tensions have reduced demand to and from the region and disrupted established routings, and the sharp rise in jet fuel prices has added pressure on airline costs. EUROCONTROL estimates that the crisis will remove about **110,000 movements** in 2026 compared with a scenario without tensions. Substantial changes to the airlines' winter schedules add to the uncertainty.

The figures in the [full report](https://www.eurocontrol.int/sites/default/files/2026-10/eurocontrol-seven-year-forecast-2026-2032-report.pdf) put the year in perspective: 11.046 million flights in 2025 (+3.9%), which was almost exactly the 2019 level of 11.085 million, and **11.234 million** expected in 2026 in the base scenario, about 1% above 2019. The margin around the 2026 figure is ±0.6 percentage points.

## A rebound in 2027, from a lower base

In the base scenario, traffic grows by **3.2%** in 2027 to about **11.6 million flights**, then by between 1.8% and 2.6% a year, reaching about **12.8 million flights in 2032**. The average growth over 2026-2032, **2.1% a year**, is unchanged from the spring forecast, but the absolute numbers are slightly lower because the starting point is lower.

{{figure:growth.svg}}

The base scenario assumes that the situation in the Middle East stabilises by the end of the first quarter of 2027 and that oil prices ease, with slightly weaker economic growth in 2026 and 2027. The low scenario assumes no resolution before the end of the first quarter of 2028 and prolonged high fuel prices: traffic then barely moves, at about 11.3 million flights a year from 2027 to 2032. The high scenario, with faster easing and enough airline and airport capacity, reaches **14.5 million flights in 2032**. EUROCONTROL advises users of the forecast to consider the whole range between low and high, not the base case alone.

## Where the growth is

Southern and eastern European markets are doing better than the large north-western ones, and Türkiye and parts of the eastern Mediterranean are expected to recover strongly in 2027. For readers in Morocco, one line stands out: traffic to **North Africa remained strong** during summer 2026. The report gives no figure for Morocco itself.

The Anadolu dispatch also recalls the network's recent load: in September, an average of **35,679 flights a day**, with a peak of 37,548 flights on 4 September, and more than 2.4 million minutes of air traffic flow management (ATFM) delay, caused by capacity constraints, weather and air traffic management issues.

## Why it matters to controllers

The seven-year forecast is the reference used to size air traffic control capacity: the number of sectors that can be opened, the controllers to recruit and train, and the performance targets of the current reference period (RP4, 2025-2029), for which the report gives average growth of 2.6% a year in the base case. For controllers, the message is that the network keeps growing, more slowly than expected this year, and that the range between the scenarios is wide.
