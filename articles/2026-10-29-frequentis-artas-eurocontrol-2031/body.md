EUROCONTROL has awarded the Austrian company Frequentis a new long-term contract to maintain and develop **ARTAS**, the multi-sensor surveillance tracker that builds the air picture used by controllers across most of Europe. The contract, announced on 29 October 2026 and reported by [ATC Network](https://www.atc-network.com/atc-news/frequentis/eurocontrol-awards-frequentis-new-long-term-contract-to-advance-european-air-traffic-surveillance) and in the company's [regulatory announcement](https://www.tradingview.com/news/reuters.com,2026-10-29:newsml_PexLrV2Ba:0-pta-news-frequentis-ag-eurocontrol-awards-frequentis-new-long-term-contract-to-advance-european-air-traffic-surveillance/), runs **until 2031**.

## What was signed

The award is the fifth industrial partner contract for EUROCONTROL's Centralised ARTAS Maintenance and Operational Support service, known as **CAMOS**. This edition is called CAMOS VI IP ("IP" for industrial partner). Under it, Frequentis continues to provide software support, continuous maintenance and enhancements of ARTAS for the community of air navigation service providers that use it. Frequentis has been EUROCONTROL's industrial partner on ARTAS since 2001, so the new contract extends a relationship that is now 25 years old. Neither source gives the value of the contract.

The work is carried out by Frequentis Comsoft, the group's German subsidiary, which joined Frequentis in 2016 and serves as its competence centre for the Aeronautical Message Handling System (AMHS), aeronautical information management (AIM), surveillance technologies and ATM systems. An [earlier announcement](https://www.atc-network.com/atc-news/frequentis-comsoft/eurocontrol-awards-frequentis-comsoft-further-five-year-contract-for-the-maintenance-of-artas-surveillance-tracker), published in January 2020 for the previous five-year contract (2020 to 2025), placed the company in Karlsruhe.

## What ARTAS does

A controller's radar screen does not show raw radar returns. Between the sensors and the display sits a **tracker**, the software that takes reports from many surveillance sources, works out which reports belong to which aircraft, and produces one smooth, consistent track for each flight. ARTAS (ATM surveillance Tracker And Server) is EUROCONTROL's tracker, developed on behalf of its member states. The 2020 announcement listed the sources it combines: primary and secondary surveillance radar (PSR and SSR), Mode S, Automatic Dependent Surveillance–Broadcast (ADS-B) and multilateration.

According to the 2026 announcement, ARTAS supports **about 90% of Europe's daily flight movements** and has **more than 50 operational installations**, which makes it the most widely deployed tracker in Europe. The 2020 text gave a different count, about 100 ARTAS units across 43 air traffic control centres; the two figures do not measure the same thing (units against operational installations), and neither source explains the difference.

## Why it matters to controllers

For controllers, the tracker is invisible when it works and central when it does not. The quality of the track, how quickly a new aircraft appears, how well a turn is followed, whether two sensors produce one target or two, determines how much they can trust what they see. A shared tracker across many European centres also means that adjacent units see aircraft built from the same logic, which helps at handovers between sectors and countries.

The new contract keeps one company responsible for that software through the next five years, during which the tracker must keep merging data from radar and cooperative surveillance sources such as ADS-B and multilateration. Antonio Principe, EUROCONTROL's ARTAS and SDDS services manager, described Frequentis as "a trusted industrial partner". Josef Kutschi, managing director of Frequentis Comsoft, said the award was "much more than a contract extension".

## The company

Frequentis, listed in Vienna and Frankfurt, reports about 2,900 full-time equivalent employees, a presence in more than 50 countries and products used in around 150. Its 2025 revenue was €580 million, with earnings before interest and tax of €47 million, according to the announcement.

## What comes next

The contract covers the evolution of ARTAS until 2031. The sources do not list the specific enhancements planned; the users of ARTAS, the European air navigation service providers, will see them as new software versions delivered through the CAMOS service.
