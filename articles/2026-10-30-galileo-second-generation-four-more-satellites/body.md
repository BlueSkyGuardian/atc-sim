The European Space Agency (ESA) has ordered **four more Galileo Second Generation (G2) satellites**, bringing the next generation of Europe's satellite navigation system to **16 spacecraft** under contract. The contracts were signed on 23 October in Rome with Thales Alenia Space and on 26 October in Ottobrunn, near Munich, with Airbus Defence and Space, ESA said on 27 October in its [announcement](https://www.esa.int/Applications/Satellite_navigation/Galileo/ESA_procures_four_new_Galileo_Second_Generation_satellites). [Airbus](https://airbus.com/en/newsroom/press-releases/2026-10-airbus-awarded-contract-for-two-additional-galileo-satellites) confirmed the same day that it will build two of them.

## What was ordered

ESA is acting on behalf of the EU Agency for the Space Programme (EUSPA), under a delegation from the European Commission. The two manufacturers have been under contract since 2021 to build six G2 satellites each; these extensions add to those orders. Airbus says its two new satellites come on top of its existing six, and the Thales Alenia Space chief executive, quoted by ESA, also speaks of "these two additional satellites".

The four new spacecraft are to be identical to the twelve already in development and are scheduled for delivery in 2031, to be launched soon afterwards. For the first batch, ESA plans integration and flight testing in 2027 and the **first G2 launch in 2028**.

## What changes with the second generation

According to ESA and Airbus, the G2 satellites carry:

- **fully digital navigation payloads** that can be reconfigured in orbit to support new signals and services;
- a better-performing navigation antenna, including higher-strength versions that Airbus says will improve accuracy;
- an enhanced **atomic clock** configuration;
- **inter-satellite links**, so that the satellites can communicate with and cross-check each other;
- **anti-jamming and anti-spoofing** capabilities to protect Galileo's signals, which Airbus highlights in its release.

Each Airbus satellite weighs more than two tonnes and is designed for a 15-year life. ESA says the second generation will make Galileo more resilient and its positioning, navigation and timing more robust and reliable.

## The constellation today

Galileo has served users since its Open Service began in 2016, and ESA describes it as the world's most precise satellite navigation system. The first generation counts **38 satellites**, all but four of which have been launched. The G2 spacecraft will join and eventually replace them.

## Why it matters in the control room

Satellite navigation is now part of daily work in air traffic control: area navigation (RNAV) routes, performance-based navigation (PBN) approaches and the position reports that feed surveillance all depend on it. The European Union Aviation Safety Agency (EASA) and the US Federal Aviation Administration (FAA) have both seen a notable increase in satellite-signal jamming and spoofing since 2022, mostly near conflict zones, with EASA citing the Mediterranean, the Black Sea, the Middle East, the Baltic Sea and the Arctic, as [Runway Girl Network](https://runwaygirlnetwork.com/2026/07/easa-updates-aircraft-gnss-jamming-and-spoofing-guidance/) reported when EASA updated its guidance in July. Spoofing, which feeds aircraft a false position, is the harder of the two to detect. A satellite system designed from the start with protection against jamming and spoofing, and able to take on new signals through reprogramming in orbit, is meant to make the signals themselves harder to disrupt; the receivers on board aircraft and the aviation standards that govern their use will also have to follow before pilots and controllers see the benefit.

ESA's Laurent Jaffart called the order "an investment in Europe's ability to navigate its own future, independently and with confidence". Neither ESA nor Airbus gives the value of the contracts.

## What comes next

The next milestones are the integration and flight tests of the first G2 satellites in 2027 and the first launch in 2028. The four satellites ordered this month are due in 2031.
