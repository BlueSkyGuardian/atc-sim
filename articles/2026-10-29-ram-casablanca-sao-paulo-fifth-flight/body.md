Royal Air Maroc (RAM) has added a fifth weekly non-stop flight between Casablanca Mohammed V (ICAO: GMMN) and São Paulo-Guarulhos (SBGR). The first departure from Casablanca on the new frequency left on **Wednesday 28 October 2026 at 15:30** local time, and the first return departure from Brazil was scheduled for **Thursday 29 October at 00:25** local time, according to [Aeronautique.ma](https://www.aeronautique.ma/Royal-Air-Maroc-passe-a-cinq-vols-hebdomadaires-entre-Casablanca-et-Sao-Paulo_a6126.html) and the Brazil-Arab News Agency [ANBA](https://anba.com.br/royal-air-maroc-estreia-quinto-voo-casablanca-sp/).

## The new weekly pattern

Both sources publish the same days of operation:

* **Casablanca to São Paulo:** Mondays, Tuesdays, Wednesdays, Thursdays and Saturdays.
* **São Paulo to Casablanca:** Tuesdays, Wednesdays, Thursdays, Fridays and Sundays.

Neither report names the aircraft type, the seat capacity or the flight numbers, and neither gives passenger or load-factor figures for the route. What they do say is that the route has been ramping up since RAM resumed it in **December 2024**, after it had been suspended during the pandemic, and that this fifth frequency continues that growth.

RAM presents the timing as deliberate: the extra flight arrives at the start of the European winter season, which the airline describes as an important period for Brazilian travel agencies and tour operators. From Casablanca, passengers from Brazil can connect to the airline's network in Europe, Africa, the Middle East and Asia.

## A launch with a football flavour

The airline chose a well-known passenger for the first São Paulo–Casablanca rotation on the new frequency: Cafu, the former Brazil international and two-time World Cup winner, travelling with his wife, the journalist Mariah Morais. ANBA reports that their trip is being filmed for a three-episode mini-series. Othman Baba, RAM's regional director for South America, told ANBA that welcoming the football star at this new stage was "a special joy" for the airline. Both sources link the operation to the 2030 FIFA World Cup, which Morocco will co-host with Spain and Portugal.

## What it means for controllers

For Moroccan air traffic control, the change is small in volume but visible in shape. Five weekly rotations mean ten long-haul movements a week at Casablanca Mohammed V on this one city pair: five departures and five arrivals. On the first day, the Casablanca departure was timed at 15:30, an afternoon slot for a long-haul departure at Morocco's main hub.

The great-circle distance between the two airports, calculated from their published coordinates, is about **7,540 km**, almost all of it over the South Atlantic. Flights on this route therefore spend most of their time in oceanic airspace, where procedural control, position reports and datalink matter more than radar coverage. For trainees, the route is a good example of the transition between a busy terminal area and an oceanic sector: a departure from Casablanca climbs out through the terminal control area before handing over to the en-route and then oceanic units along the route.

## Background

The route itself is not new. RAM flew to São Paulo before the pandemic, suspended the service, and brought it back in December 2024; both sources describe the fifth frequency as the continuation of a ramp-up since then. Aeronautique.ma notes that the airline presents the increase as a way to strengthen its offer between Morocco and Brazil, while ANBA, which covers trade between Brazil and the Arab world, focuses on the connections it opens for Brazilian travellers via Casablanca.

## What comes next

The two reports give no date for further growth on the route. The next indicators to watch are ONDA's monthly traffic figures for Casablanca Mohammed V, which will show how the airport's traffic evolves through the winter, and RAM's winter schedule for its other long-haul routes.
