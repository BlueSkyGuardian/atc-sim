Global air cargo demand rose **7.7% year on year in September**, with growth in every region, but jet fuel cost **more than twice as much as a year earlier**, the International Air Transport Association (IATA) said on 29 October in its monthly [market analysis](https://www.iata.org/en/pressroom/2026-releases/10-29-air-cargo-demand-accelerates-september/). The figures were also reported the same day by [Air Cargo News](https://www.aircargonews.net/data/2026/10/iata-air-cargo-demand-up-more-than-7-in-september/).

## The headline numbers

Demand, measured in cargo tonne-kilometres (CTK), grew 7.7% compared with September 2025, and 8.6% on international routes. Capacity, in available cargo tonne-kilometres (ACTK), grew much less: 2.7% overall and 3.3% internationally. With demand growing faster than capacity, the global cargo load factor rose 2.2 percentage points to 47.9%.

By region of airline registration:

- **North American** carriers grew fastest, at 11.8%, with capacity almost flat (+0.8%) and a load factor of 43.9%.
- **Asia-Pacific** airlines, which carry 35.8% of the world's air cargo, grew 8.6%.
- **Latin American** carriers grew 6.7%, but their capacity grew faster still (+7.6%).
- **African** airlines grew 4.7% while cutting capacity by 1.2%, lifting their load factor 2.7 points to 47.0%. They carry 2.1% of world traffic.
- **European** carriers grew 4.5% with 1.6% less capacity, reaching the highest load factor of any region, 54.6%.
- **Middle Eastern** carriers grew least, at 3.8%, while adding 7.0% of capacity, so their load factor fell 1.4 points to 44.0%.

## Trade lanes: the Pacific up, the Gulf down

The picture by trade lane is more contrasted. The Asia–North America lane, the largest with 23.5% of industry traffic, grew 17.0%, its eighth consecutive month of growth. Within Asia, traffic grew 10.2% (35 months in a row), Europe–North America 9.3% and Europe–Asia 6.0%, the latter now growing for 43 consecutive months. Africa–Asia returned to growth with 2.6%.

Two lanes through the Gulf kept shrinking: Middle East–Asia fell 10.2% and Europe–Middle East 18.0%, both for the seventh month running. IATA does not give the reasons in its release; airspace restrictions in the region have been in force for months, including the European Union Aviation Safety Agency's (EASA) advice to avoid the Tehran flight information region (FIR) at all levels, extended in late September to 16 November, as [Avi-Go](https://avi-go.com/news/articles/ca06a4019e5652b0c3496a130bab663c) reported.

## Fuel: the real problem

The most striking figure is not in the demand data. Jet fuel prices rose 17.5% from August to September and were **103.4% higher than a year earlier**. "Jet fuel prices have more than doubled compared with a year ago, and yields have not kept pace," said IATA's Marie Owens Thomsen, adding that there is no shortage of goods to ship but that doing so profitably is getting harder.

The wider economy still supports demand. Global trade grew 3.6% year on year in August, its 42nd consecutive month of expansion, and the global manufacturing output index rose to 53.8, with new export orders at 51.9, both at five-year highs, according to IATA.

## Why it matters to controllers

Cargo flights make up much of the night traffic at many airports. Strong demand with flat or falling capacity in Europe, Africa and North America means fuller aircraft rather than more flights, so the growth shows up more in load factors than in movements on controllers' screens. The trade-lane figures, by contrast, point to a change in where the freight flies: less of it between the Gulf, Asia and Europe, and more across the Pacific and the North Atlantic.

## What comes next

IATA's October cargo figures are due next month. High fuel prices are the factor to watch: they weigh on airlines' decisions to keep, add or cut freighter capacity over the winter.
