Italy's transport sector came through Friday 30 October under a government order postponing walkouts, but its air navigation service provider, **ENAV**, now faces a series of air traffic control strikes in November: a local stoppage at **Bari on 6 November**, a **national four-hour strike on 21 November** and a **24-hour strike at Milan Malpensa on 22 November**.

## What happened to the 30 October strike

The grassroots union USB had called a 24-hour general strike for 30 October, including transport. On **27 October**, the Ministry of Infrastructure and Transport issued **ordinance no. 204 T**, postponing the transport part of the strike (rail, local public transport, **air**, maritime and taxi services, among others) to a date still to be set, according to [Trasporti-Italia](https://www.trasporti-italia.com/mobilita/scioperi/sciopero-30-ottobre-2026-rinvio-per-trasporti-treni-aerei-bus/1533162/). The reason was the **"franchigia"**, the protected period around All Saints' and All Souls' days, which Trasporti-Italia gives as **30 October to 5 November**, during which transport services must be provided in full. Italy's strike guarantee commission had flagged the problem in its resolution 26/320 of 22 October.

USB contested the order and said it would maintain the strike in transport too; Trasporti-Italia reports that the union warned workers of possible sanctions and offered legal assistance to those who asked for it. [Il Post](https://www.ilpost.it/2026/10/29/sciopero-trasporti-precettazione-disagi/) expected limited disruption: "Con ogni probabilità però la partecipazione sarà limitata ai soli delegati del sindacato" ("in all likelihood, participation will be limited to the union's delegates"), it wrote on 29 October, adding that several transport companies, Trenitalia among them, announced normal services. The strike stayed in force in other sectors such as schools and healthcare.

The ATC SIM newsroom has not found an official account of how air traffic ran on the day, and the sources do not say whether the ordinance also covered the separate four-hour stoppage of ENAV and Techno Sky staff that the Uiltrasporti union had called for the same Friday.

## The November calendar

According to [Missionline](https://www.missionline.it/sciopero-aerei-novembre-2026-si-parte-il-6-da-bari-tutte-le-date-e-gli-orari/), which lists the air transport strikes notified for November:

- **Friday 6 November, 13:00–17:00**: ENAV staff at **Bari (LIBD)**, called by the RSA FAST-CONFSAL-AV union representatives, a local strike;
- **Saturday 21 November, 13:00–17:00**: a **national** strike of ENAV staff, called by FILT-CGIL and UGL-TA;
- **Sunday 22 November, 00:01–24:00**: ENAV staff at **Milan Malpensa (LIMC)**, called by RSA FAST-CONFSAL-AV. Missionline notes that flights to and from Bari are guaranteed on that day;
- **Sunday 22 November**: a 24-hour strike of Sicuritalia IVRI security staff at Venice Marco Polo airport, called by FISASCAT-CISL.

Missionline stresses that these are not general walkouts of all airlines, and that the effect on each flight has to be checked with the airline and the airport.

## Why it matters to controllers

For neighbouring units and for the network, the three ENAV dates are what count. A four-hour national stoppage on a Saturday afternoon, and a full day at Malpensa can mean regulations, rerouting and late changes to flight plans on flows across the Alps and the Mediterranean, including traffic between Italy and North Africa. Italian rules guarantee a minimum service during air transport strikes, and notified strikes can still be called off or reduced, so the actual impact will only be known in the days before each date.
