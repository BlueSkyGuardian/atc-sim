Italy's air navigation service provider faces another strike on **Friday 30 October**. The Uiltrasporti union has called a **four-hour nationwide stoppage** of staff at **ENAV**, the national air navigation service provider, and **Techno Sky**, its subsidiary that maintains air traffic management systems, citing "the lack of answers on numerous open disputes" and stalled negotiations with the company, according to [Il Sole 24 Ore](https://en.ilsole24ore.com/art/strikes-with-two-black-fridays-to-go-in-a-months-time-AJma66tB) and [Askanews](https://askanews.it/2026/10/25/scioperi-il-30-ottobre-e-il-20-novembre-due-venerdi-neri-di-proteste/). Neither report gives the hours of the stoppage.

## A crowded Friday, and a government order

The ENAV action falls on a day already loaded with industrial action. The grassroots union USB has called a 24-hour general strike for 30 October which, according to [Sbircia la Notizia](https://www.sbircialanotizia.it/articoli/2026/10/26/sciopero-30-ottobre-fk9he/), covers air transport from 00:00 to 23:59. Other general strikes the same day, called by Cobas and allied unions, exclude transport.

The authorities have pushed back. On 22 October, Italy's strike guarantee commission (Commissione di garanzia) warned the infrastructure and transport minister of a "well-founded danger of serious and imminent harm" to freedom of movement, noting that a strike across the whole transport sector would leave travellers with no alternative. Sbircia la Notizia also reports that ENAC, the civil aviation authority, places the period from 30 October to 5 November inside the protected holiday period around All Souls' Day, when transport strikes are normally barred.

On 28 October, [QuiFinanza](https://quifinanza.it/info-utili/scioperi/precettazione-sciopero-generale-30-ottobre-2026/1021624/) reported that the ministry had issued an ordinance asking for the air transport strike to be postponed, citing the rule that protects travel for the commemoration of the dead on 31 October and 1 November. USB called the order "illegitimate" and said it would strike anyway and accept the sanctions. QuiFinanza does not say whether the ordinance also covers Uiltrasporti's four-hour ENAV and Techno Sky action, so the exact scope of Friday's disruption remained unclear at the time of writing.

## Protected flights

If air transport strikes go ahead, Italian rules guarantee a minimum service. Flights scheduled to depart between **07:00 and 10:00** and between **18:00 and 21:00** local time must operate, and ENAC publishes the list of guaranteed flights. Sbircia la Notizia adds that international arrivals within 30 minutes of those windows, one intercontinental departure per continent and a daily link to the islands are also protected.

Ground handling is affected as well. Staff of Toscana Aeroporti, GH Toscana and Consulta at **Pisa** and **Florence** have called a 24-hour strike on Friday, and La Lucente handling staff at **Bologna** are due to strike from 12:00 to 16:00 on Thursday 29 October, according to [TravelTourister](https://www.traveltourister.com/news/italy-aviation-strike-october-29-30-2026-bologna-la-lucente-enav-atc-tuscany-pisa-florence-half-term-eu261-uk261/).

## More dates in November

Friday is not the end of it. Sbircia la Notizia lists a four-hour ENAV stoppage at **Bari** airport on 6 November and a four-hour ENAV strike from 13:00 to 17:00 on **21 November**, called by Filt-Cgil and Ugl Trasporto Aereo. A strike calendar compiled by [MyFlyRight](https://myflyright.com/blog/europe-flight-strikes-october-and-november-2026/) as of 15 October also listed a 24-hour ENAV strike at Milan Malpensa on 22 November. The calendars do not all match, and strikes in Italy are often modified, postponed or withdrawn at short notice, so the official register remains the reference.

## Why it matters

ENAV handles the en-route and approach traffic over Italy and the towers at its main airports. For crews and passengers on routes between Morocco and Italian airports, Friday's walkouts mean possible cancellations outside the protected windows. For controllers elsewhere in Europe, Italian strikes typically translate into regulations and reroutings on flows that cross Italian airspace. Under EU Regulation 261/2004, passengers whose flights are cancelled keep the right to a refund or rerouting and to care while they wait; cash compensation applies only in some cases.

The dispute behind the ENAV and Techno Sky action has not been resolved, and the unions have now filed several dates. Whether Friday's stoppage goes ahead as called, or is shortened or moved under the ministry's order, will only be clear in the final hours before the strike.
