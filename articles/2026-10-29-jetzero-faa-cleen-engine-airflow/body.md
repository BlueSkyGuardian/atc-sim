The US Federal Aviation Administration (FAA) has awarded **$5.3 million** to JetZero, the Californian start-up developing a blended-wing-body airliner, to measure in flight how air reaches engines mounted on top of an all-wing aircraft. The award, announced by the company on 28 October 2026 in a [press release](https://www.prnewswire.com/news-releases/jetzero-awarded-5-3m-in-faa-cleen-iv-302921712.html) and detailed the same day by [AIN](https://www.ainonline.com/aviation-news/futureflight/2026-10-28/faa-funds-jetzero-study-top-mounted-engine-airflow), comes from the fourth phase of the FAA's **CLEEN** programme.

## What will be measured

The money pays for instrumentation on the engines and nacelles of **Jet1**, JetZero's full-scale demonstrator of its Z4 airliner. Sensors will record the airflow at each engine's inlet and just behind its fan during take-off, climb and cruise, and during ground operations in crosswinds. JetZero will share the data with the FAA.

The point of the test is the layout. On the Z4, the engines sit on top of the aircraft's rear body rather than under the wings. Air reaching them has first flowed over the wide, lifting body of the aircraft, and the data will show how it arrives at the fan in each phase of flight and in a crosswind on the ground. JetZero argues that measuring this on a real aircraft in a representative environment is cheaper and more realistic than engine test rigs or ground simulations.

## The CLEEN programme

CLEEN (Continuous Lower Energy, Emissions and Noise) is the FAA's main public-private partnership for maturing aircraft technologies that reduce fuel burn, emissions and community noise, and for speeding their entry into the fleet. AIN reports that Phase IV was launched in September 2026 and that the FAA's January draft solicitation foresaw up to $25 million for the whole phase, against $100 million or more for each of the three earlier five-year phases. The other Phase IV agreements, two-year cost-shared deals whose values were not disclosed, went to Boeing, GE Aerospace, Heart Aerospace and Honeywell Aerospace.

## The aircraft

The Z4 is designed as a 250-seat airliner with a range of up to 5,000 nautical miles, aimed at the middle of the market. JetZero claims it will be up to 50% more fuel-efficient than tube-and-wing aircraft of the same class using the same engine technology, and that placing the engines on top will reduce noise for communities around airports. Both claims are the company's; they have not yet been demonstrated in flight.

According to AIN, Jet1 is being assembled by Scaled Composites, a Northrop Grumman subsidiary, at Mojave Air & Space Port in California, and was 40% complete in June 2026. It will fly with two Pratt & Whitney PW2040 turbofans; JetZero has not chosen an engine supplier for the production Z4. The **first flight of Jet1 is planned for the fourth quarter of 2027**, with entry into service of the Z4 targeted for the early 2030s. The company broke ground on a factory in Greensboro, North Carolina, in June 2026.

On the commercial side, United Airlines has a conditional agreement for up to 100 Z4s with options for 100 more, linked in part to Jet1 flying in 2027, and Gulf Air signed a letter of intent for an undisclosed number at the Farnborough Airshow in July 2026. Delta Air Lines and Japan Airlines are advising on the design and operations.

## Why it matters to controllers

A blended-wing body is a very different shape from today's airliners, and a new shape eventually raises practical questions for air traffic control: wake turbulence categories, approach speeds, the space the aircraft occupies on stands and taxiways, and how it behaves in crosswinds. None of these values is public yet; they will come from flight testing, which is exactly what this award starts to fund. For now the step is modest, $5.3 million for sensors, but it ties public research money to measurements on a full-scale demonstrator rather than a model.

## What comes next

The next milestone is the completion of Jet1 at Mojave and its first flight, planned for late 2027. The data from the CLEEN-funded instrumentation will be gathered once the demonstrator flies.
