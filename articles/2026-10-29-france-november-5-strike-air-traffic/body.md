France's main trade union confederations have called a nationwide day of strikes and demonstrations for **Thursday 5 November 2026**. Because French air traffic controllers are civil servants of the Direction générale de l'aviation civile (DGAC), the civil-service strike notices filed for that day cover them. But one week before the date, the picture for air traffic control is still unclear, and the sources do not all describe it the same way.

{{figure:map.svg}}

## What has been called

According to [Volclaim](https://www.volclaim.com/actualite-aerienne/greve-5-novembre-2026-vols-aeroports.html) and [Airkarma](https://airkarma.fr/actualites/greve-5-novembre-2026-avion-aeroports), the CFDT, CGT, FO, Unsa and FSU issued a joint call on 5 October. They oppose the government's draft budget for 2027 and are asking for higher wages, a higher minimum wage and higher pensions. The call covers private-sector employees as well as public servants.

In the public sector, 24-hour notices have been filed; Volclaim reports a notice from UFFA-CFDT dated 19 October covering state, territorial and hospital civil servants. In air transport, [SUD Aérien Solidaires](https://www.sudaerien.fr/articles/2026-10-19-tract-sud-aerien-greve-du-5-novembre-2026) published a leaflet on 19 October calling on ramp, terminal, handling, security, cleaning and maintenance staff to stop work, for wage increases, a 32-hour week and more hiring. It names no airport.

## Where the sources differ

The two passenger-rights sites read the situation for controllers differently:

* **Airkarma** (26 October) writes that controllers, as DGAC civil servants, are covered by the civil-service notices, that public-sector notices must be filed by Friday 30 October, and that under a 2023 law each controller must declare by **noon on Tuesday 3 November** whether they will strike. It adds that, as of 26 October, the DGAC had not asked airlines to reduce their schedules.
* **Volclaim** (updated 27 October) writes that none of the sources it consulted reported a strike notice aimed specifically at pilots, cabin crew or air traffic controllers, and that no reduced flight programme was known.

Both statements can be true at once: a general civil-service notice is not the same as a call from a controllers' union. What matters operationally is the number of individual declarations, which only the DGAC will know after the Tuesday deadline.

## The 29 September precedent

The last civil-service strike day, on 29 September, gives an idea of what the DGAC can do. According to Reuters, as published by [Euronext](https://live.euronext.com/en/financial-news/french-aviation-regulator-cancels-flights-due-civil-servants-strike), the DGAC asked airlines to cancel **5% of flights at Paris-Charles de Gaulle between 07:00 and 20:00** and **25% at Marseille-Provence between 16:00 and midnight**, and warned of possible disruption at Nantes, Strasbourg, Limoges, Lourdes and Pau. Traffic at other French airports was expected to be normal. Airkarma notes that the request was made the evening before.

Requests of this kind come after the controllers' individual declarations are known, so that airlines can cancel flights in advance rather than see delays build up during the day.

## Why it matters beyond France

French airspace sits on the main routes between Morocco and the rest of Europe. On 29 September, however, the DGAC's requests were limited to two airports, and Reuters reported that traffic at the other French airports was expected to be normal. For crews and dispatchers on Morocco–France routes, the practical point is to watch for a DGAC announcement on 3 or 4 November.

For passengers, both sites recall that under the EU's air passenger rights regulation (EU261), air traffic control strikes are generally treated as extraordinary circumstances, which exempt airlines from paying compensation, although the duty of care (meals, and accommodation if an overnight stay is needed) still applies.

## What to watch

* **Friday 30 October:** deadline for public-sector strike notices, according to Airkarma.
* **Tuesday 3 November, noon:** deadline for controllers' individual declarations.
* **3–4 November:** a possible DGAC request to airlines to reduce schedules, airport by airport.
* **Thursday 5 November:** the strike day.

Separately, readers should not confuse this French action with the four-hour strike at ENAV, the Italian air navigation service provider, called for Friday 30 October, which this newsroom reported yesterday.
