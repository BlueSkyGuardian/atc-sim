Ryanair's chief executive Eddie Wilson was in Rabat on Tuesday 27 October to present the airline's winter 2026 programme for Morocco, as part of its partnership with the Moroccan National Tourism Office (ONMT), [Le Brief](https://www.lebrief.ma/ryanair-renforce-sa-presence-au-maroc-avec-156-lignes-100171357/) and [Premiumtravelnews](https://premiumtravelnews.com/2026/10/28/ryanair-muscle-sa-presence-au-maroc-avec-53-millions-de-sieges-hiver-2026/) report. The headline numbers: **5.3 million seats**, about 600,000 more than last winter (+12%), on **156 routes** to **14 countries**, serving **13 Moroccan airports**.

## What was presented

According to both reports, the programme includes **17 new routes** and a **new base at Rabat-Salé**, Ryanair's fifth in Morocco. Two additional aircraft are being assigned to the country, bringing the fleet based in Morocco to **16 aircraft**. The airline put its cumulative investment at 1.6 billion US dollars and says it supports more than 8,500 local jobs; those figures come from the company.

The plan itself is not new. [Air Journal](https://www.air-journal.fr/2026-08-09-ryanair-au-maroc-un-programme-hivernal-record-pour-relier-le-royaume-a-14-pays-europeens-5276824.html) reported the same programme on 9 August (5.3 million seats, 156 routes, 17 new, the Rabat base and 16 based aircraft) and listed some of the new routes: Rabat to Stockholm and Kraków, Marrakech to Wrocław, and Agadir to Milan, Nuremberg, Bratislava and Gdańsk. It said that two of the based aircraft would be at Rabat and that 480 pilot and cabin crew jobs were included. What Tuesday's visit added was confirmation, at the start of the season, that the programme stands.

Le Brief and Premiumtravelnews also name the cities that the partners expect to benefit from better air access beyond the main tourist hubs: **Fès, Tétouan, Essaouira, Ouarzazate, Dakhla and Nador**. Neither article gives a date for the opening of the Rabat base or the route-by-route frequencies.

## Why Rabat matters

Rabat-Salé (GMME) is the fifth-busiest airport in the country, with 1,902,312 passengers between January and September 2026 according to ONDA's latest figures (see our separate article on national traffic). A base usually changes the shape of an airport's day, since aircraft that stay overnight tend to leave in the first wave of the morning and come back in the evening; the reports do not give Ryanair's Rabat schedule.

The airport is also changing on the ground. On 23 October, [Morocco World News](https://www.moroccoworldnews.com/2026/10/343516/rabat-sale-airport-rolls-out-automated-self-bag-drop-in-national-deployment/) reported that Airports of Morocco (the new brand of ONDA) had installed six automated self bag drop kiosks in Rabat-Salé's Terminal 2 for Ryanair passengers, the first step of a national roll-out after Royal Air Maroc's introduction of the system at Casablanca. ONDA's Airports 2030 plan, presented at the [Marrakech Airshow](https://www.aeronautique.ma/Marrakech-Airshow-2026-l-ONDA-met-en-scene-sa-transformation-sous-la-marque-Airports-of-Morocco_a6117.html) in early October, lists a new 71,000 m² terminal at Rabat-Salé with a capacity target of 7 million passengers, dated 2026.

## Smaller airports, new routes

None of the cities on the list appears among the five busiest airports in ONDA's figures, so even a few new weekly flights are a visible change for the units that handle them. Essaouira (GMMI) is also gaining a domestic link of its own: [Morocco World News](https://www.moroccoworldnews.com/2026/10/342280/royal-air-maroc-to-launch-direct-casablanca-essaouira-flights-on-november-1/) reported that Royal Air Maroc will open a twice-weekly Casablanca–Essaouira service on 1 November, on Wednesdays and Sundays, bringing the national carrier's domestic network to 28 routes.

## What comes next

The test will be in the monthly traffic figures for November to March, which will show how much of the 600,000 additional seats turns into passengers, and how the new flights are spread between the main airports and the regional ones.
