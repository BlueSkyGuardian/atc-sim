The US Federal Aviation Administration (FAA) plans to deploy **Mobile Clearance** in early 2027: a service that lets general-aviation (GA) pilots request and receive their instrument flight rules (IFR) departure clearance as text in their flight-planning app, instead of reading it back by voice on the clearance delivery or ground frequency. FAA programme managers presented the latest status on 21 October at the NBAA-BACE business aviation convention in Las Vegas, as reported on 28 October by [Aviation Week](https://aviationweek.com/business-aviation/safety-ops-regulation/faa-mobile-clearances-begin-2027-ga) and [Avi-Go](https://avi-go.com/news/articles/ddc7de514b8e6faa25975ff621f212a2).

## How it works

The idea is familiar to anyone who has worked a tower with datalink. Airliners equipped with VHF datalink radios already receive departure clearances through controller-pilot data link communications (CPDLC-DCL) or the pre-departure clearance (PDC) service. Mobile Clearance offers the same function to pilots who have no datalink avionics, through their personal device and an internet connection. For now, the two apps offering it are **ForeFlight** and **Garmin Pilot**, according to the [FAA's programme page](https://www.faa.gov/air_traffic/technology/mobile_clearance).

The pilot presses a button in the app to request the clearance, receives it as text, and can answer with Accept or Wilco. On first contact with the tower or ground controller, the pilot simply states that they have their "mobile clearance". The FAA says the text format reduces the risk of readback errors and gives controllers better situational awareness. Unlike PDC, the service also handles revised clearances and a "contact me" message, and works for any aircraft the pilot operates, not only aircraft with registered airline callsigns. It is currently limited to Part 91 (private) and Part 135 (commuter and on-demand) operators.

On the controller side, the system is cloud-based. The controller interface is a web page with facility-specific settings, and the tower hardware is commercial off-the-shelf: a 24-inch monitor, a mini PC, a keyboard, a mouse and an internet connection. Flight-plan data come through the FAA's System Wide Information Management (SWIM) data-sharing backbone. "Getting that in text form is a beautiful thing," Paul Diffenderfer, the FAA's Mobile Clearance technical lead, told the convention, according to Aviation Week.

## Where it is being tested

The operational evaluation opened in May 2026 at three airports in the Houston area: Sugar Land Regional (SGR), Scholes International at Galveston (GLS) and David Wayne Hooks Memorial (DWH). It was extended over the summer to Appleton International (ATW) in Wisconsin and New Century AirCenter (IXD) in Kansas. The FAA's page lists six further airports for the autumn: College Station (CLL), Kansas City Downtown (MKC), Johnson County Executive at Olathe (OJC), Austin Executive (EDC), Georgetown (GTU) and San Marcos (HYI). An **IFR cancellation** function, which lets pilots cancel their IFR flight plan from the app after landing at an uncontrolled airport, is being introduced through Houston's terminal radar approach control (TRACON, I90).

The scale of the problem the FAA is targeting is large. According to [World Airnews](https://worldairnews.co.za/faa-confirms-nationwide-rollout-plan-for-mobile-clearance-ending-evaluation-in-february-2027-as-digital-ifr-departure-clearances-expand/), which reported the FAA's announcement at EAA AirVenture in July, more than half of the 44,000 daily IFR runway departure requests in the United States are still made by voice. The project began with the research organisation MITRE in 2016, and simulations with pilots and controllers started at the FAA's William J. Hughes Technical Center in 2024.

## Timeline: a small discrepancy

The sources do not give exactly the same end date for the evaluation. Aviation Week and Avi-Go, reporting the October presentation, say it is expected to run until the end of 2026. The FAA's own page and the July reports give February 2027, followed by nationwide deployment. All agree that the service is meant to reach airports across the country during 2027, including selected non-towered airports for IFR cancellation; an IFR release function for non-towered airports is planned for a later phase, with no date.

## Why it matters for controllers elsewhere

For tower and ground controllers, every clearance moved from voice to text frees frequency time and removes one opportunity for a readback error. The FAA's choice of cheap, internet-based hardware and pilots' own tablets is what makes it interesting outside the United States: it reaches the light-aircraft and business-aviation traffic that will never carry datalink avionics.

In Morocco, datalink is still being deployed. EUROCONTROL's 2024 Local Single Sky Implementation (LSSIP) report for Morocco, as summarised by [Barlamane](https://barlamane.com/fr/le-maroc-accelere-la-transformation-de-son-espace-aerien-et-structure-une-montee-en-capacite-nationale-selon-le-rapport-lssip-2024/), put the implementation of air-ground datalink services at 34% and listed CPDLC among the projects in progress. There is no announced plan for an app-based clearance service in Morocco; the US evaluation will show whether the model works at scale.
