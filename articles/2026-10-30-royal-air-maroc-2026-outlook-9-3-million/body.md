Royal Air Maroc (RAM) expects to end 2026 with **9.3 million passengers** and revenue of **more than 25 billion dirhams**, according to the report on public establishments and enterprises that accompanies Morocco's draft 2027 Finance Bill (PLF 2027). The figures were reported on 26 and 27 October by [La Vie éco](https://www.lavieeco.com/affaires/entreprises/ram-prevoit-de-cloturer-2026-avec-93-millions-de-passagers-et-plus-de-25-mmdh-de-ca/) and [Akhir Saa](https://www.akhirsaa.com/%D8%A7%D9%84%D8%AE%D8%B7%D9%88%D8%B7-%D8%A7%D9%84%D9%85%D9%84%D9%83%D9%8A%D8%A9-%D8%A7%D9%84%D9%85%D8%BA%D8%B1%D8%A8%D9%8A%D8%A9-%D8%AA%D8%AA%D9%88%D9%82%D8%B9-%D8%AA%D8%AC%D8%A7%D9%88%D8%B2-25-%D9%85/). The document does not name a spokesperson; the numbers come from the state's own annual review of the companies it owns.

## A strong 2025, a harder start to 2026

The report gives RAM's 2025 accounts in detail. Revenue reached about 21.53 billion dirhams, up 8% on 2024, which the report notes was achieved despite growing competition from low-cost airlines. Operating income rose 168% to 1.625 billion dirhams, from 605 million a year earlier, and net profit rose 182% to about 1.336 billion dirhams, from 473 million. At group level, consolidated net profit reached about 2.1 billion dirhams (+154%), a result the report describes as unprecedented.

The national carrier also spent much more: its investment programme reached about 3.58 billion dirhams in 2025, against 291 million in 2024. Debt rose by nearly 15% to about 6.93 billion dirhams, excluding advances in partners' current accounts.

The first months of 2026 were less comfortable. At the end of April, revenue was up 17% at just over 11 billion dirhams, but the company recorded a net loss of 71 million dirhams, against a loss of 58 million a year earlier. The report attributes the loss to geopolitical crises; in May, RAM had temporarily suspended several African and European routes, citing higher jet fuel prices and operating costs, as [Asharq Al-Awsat](https://english.aawsat.com/business/5276768-morocco%E2%80%99s-royal-air-maroc-scales-back-flights-due-fuel-costs) reported at the time. The forecast of more than 25 billion dirhams for the full year therefore assumes a strong second half.

## The 2037 plan behind the numbers

The forecasts sit inside the development contract signed between the state and RAM on 11 July 2023, which runs to 2037. Its targets, as restated in the report:

- a fleet growing from **50 to 200 aircraft**;
- more than **100 new international destinations** and **46 new domestic routes**;
- revenue of **94 billion dirhams**, **31.6 million passengers** and an average seat load factor of 82%.

The gap between 9.3 million passengers this year and 31.6 million in 2037 shows the scale of the plan: more than three times the traffic in a little over a decade. The report also says that fuel-efficiency measures and newer aircraft avoided 15,596 tonnes of CO2 emissions in 2025, and that RAM aims for carbon neutrality by 2050.

## What it means for Moroccan air traffic control

RAM's growth is only part of the picture. Moroccan airports as a whole handled 29.3 million passengers in the first nine months of 2026, up 8.7%, with aircraft movements rising faster still, according to ONDA figures reported by [Hespress](https://en.hespress.com/148003-moroccos-airports-handle-29-3-million-passengers-up-8-7-in-nine-months.html).

For controllers, a national carrier that plans to multiply its fleet by four, and open dozens of new domestic routes means more traffic through Casablanca Mohammed V (GMMN), more domestic flights in the en-route sectors and more regional airports with scheduled services to handle. New domestic links, such as the Casablanca–Essaouira route that opens this weekend, are a first sign of that.

## What comes next

The PLF 2027 still has to go through Parliament. RAM's actual 2026 results will be known next year; the next public check on its traffic will be ONDA's monthly airport figures, and the delivery of the new aircraft the development contract calls for.
