European regulators have started preparing in earnest for the biggest change in air traffic controller training in more than a decade. On 27 and 28 October, the European Union Aviation Safety Agency (EASA) and EUROCONTROL held a joint workshop on **competency-based training and assessment (CBTA)** for air traffic controllers (ATCOs), aimed at the national competent authorities (NCAs) that approve and oversee training organisations, [EUROCONTROL announced](https://www.eurocontrol.int/news/easa-and-eurocontrol-joint-air-traffic-controller-competency-based-training-and-assessment) on 30 October. The workshop took place at EUROCONTROL's Aviation Learning Centre and was also reported by [The Aviation Hub](https://theaviationhub.co.uk/easa-eurocontrol-atco-cbta-workshop/).

## What changes, and when

The rules come from **Commission Implementing Regulation (EU) 2025/2143**, adopted on 23 October 2025, which amends the European ATCO licensing regulation, (EU) 2015/340, to introduce competency-based training and assessment and virtual training for controllers, [according to EASA](https://www.easa.europa.eu/newsroom-and-events/news/eu-adopts-new-regulation-modernise-air-traffic-controller-training). It completes a package based on EASA Opinion 06-2024.

From **1 January 2029**, CBTA requirements apply to initial training, to operational air traffic services (ATS) units and to ATCO training organisations. Until the end of 2028, courses may already be updated to reflect CBTA, as long as they and their training plans keep complying with the requirements in force.

In practice, CBTA moves training away from fixed hours and syllabi towards showing that a trainee has reached defined competencies, as The Aviation Hub summarises it. The training organisation Entry Point North, in an [article on the new rule](https://www.entrypointnorth.com/cbta-for-atco-training-turning-new-regulation-into-better-outcomes/), notes that the alignment runs "from basic training to unit training and continuation training", and that instructors and assessors will have to work with competency frameworks, behavioural indicators and structured assessment reports.

## What the workshop concluded

Speakers from CANSO, the GATE training organisation, EUROCONTROL's Maastricht Upper Area Control Centre (MUAC), the Belgian supervisory authority and the Irish Aviation Authority gave their views. The conclusions published by EUROCONTROL are:

- CBTA is the most significant change to ATCO training since common European rules were established, and is likely to bring benefits at network level.
- Training organisations and authorities need to talk early for timely implementation; authorities should tell organisations early what submission timelines and extra documents they expect.
- Using CBTA's flexibility fully is key to making the ATCO training pipeline more efficient.
- A more structured dialogue between authorities is needed for a harmonised implementation across the EU.

## What EASA and EUROCONTROL will do

The two organisations committed to support the authorities' initiatives on CBTA approval and oversight; to keep the discussion going over the next two years in stakeholder forums and advisory bodies; to develop a **generic introductory online course** on CBTA principles for inspectors and others; to advance EASA's **Data4Training** initiative, which aims to use European training data to improve the design and delivery of ATC courses; and to consider a specific inspector training course. The conclusions will feed into European network-level work on the **ATCO workforce challenge**.

## Why it matters

The timing is tied to traffic. EUROCONTROL's latest [seven-year forecast](https://www.eurocontrol.int/publication/eurocontrol-forecast-2026-2032) expects about 12.1 million IFR flights in Europe in 2029, the year CBTA applies, and 12.8 million in 2032 in its base scenario, against 11.05 million in 2025. The controllers who will handle that traffic are those entering training in the next few years. For training organisations, simulator providers and instructors, the message from the workshop is to start redesigning courses and assessments now rather than in 2028. Morocco is not bound by this EU regulation; for Moroccan readers, it shows the direction controller training is taking in the neighbouring European network.
