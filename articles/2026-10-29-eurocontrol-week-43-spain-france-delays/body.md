Europe's network handled an average of **32,480 flights a day** in week 43 (19 to 25 October 2026), 2.3% fewer than the week before and 0.7% more than in the same week of 2025, according to the [EUROCONTROL European Aviation Overview](https://www.eurocontrol.int/publication/eurocontrol-european-aviation-overview-2026-week-43) published on 29 October. Storms Austen and Boelo disrupted flights across northern and western Europe from 23 to 25 October. Yet the delay picture was dominated by two countries on the main routes between Morocco and the rest of Europe: **France and Spain together generated 69% of all en-route air traffic flow management (ATFM) delay**.

## Where the delay came from

En-route ATFM delay averaged about **54,000 minutes a day** over the week, 12% less than in week 42 but 6% more than a year earlier. Total ATFM delay came to 2.4 minutes per flight: 1.7 minutes en route and 0.7 minutes at airports.

EUROCONTROL attributes the en-route delay to four causes:

* **ATC capacity and staffing: 65%**;
* **weather: 20%**;
* **other causes: 10%**, partly linked to 4-FLIGHT, the new air traffic management system being introduced in France;
* **ATC disruptions: 5%**, all of it in Switzerland, after a technical failure at Skyguide.

By country, France accounted for 35% of en-route delay (20 points of it for ATC capacity, 8 for weather and 6 for other causes) and Spain for 34% (24 points for ATC capacity and 8 for weather). Germany followed with 9%, Greece with 6% and Switzerland with 5%.

## The area control centres at the top

Five area control centres (ACCs) led the ranking in minutes of en-route delay per flight: **Barcelona (2.7)**, **Marseille (2.6)**, **Madrid (1.9)**, Reims (1.1) and Brest (1.0). In shares of the network's en-route delay, Marseille came first with 18%, ahead of Barcelona (17%) and Madrid (14%).

Weather played a direct part in the Mediterranean centres. EUROCONTROL reports that storm Adam brought convective weather that complicated traffic management in Barcelona and Marseille on Monday and Tuesday, and that turbulence affected Madrid on Monday. At airports, the overview lists weather regulations at Paris Orly (cumulonimbus), Amsterdam (wind and cumulonimbus), Manchester (strong winds) and Athens (cumulonimbus).

## The Skyguide failure

The Swiss share has a single cause. In a [statement](https://www.skyguide.ch/media-centre/post/154176) dated 20 October, Skyguide reported a malfunction of the system that generates the radar display used by its controllers. Flights at Zurich and Geneva were briefly interrupted, then resumed. As a precaution, the capacity of Swiss airspace was first cut by 50%, then raised to 80% for a 24-hour stabilisation phase before the return to full capacity. Skyguide said its experts had identified the cause and that there was no indication of a cyberattack.

## Why it matters in Morocco

The flows between Morocco and Europe cross exactly the airspace that generated most of the week's delay: the Spanish centres and the French centres of Marseille, Brest and Reims. The overview also notes that traffic between Europe and North Africa rose by 3% in the week, driven in part by additional flights on the France–Morocco flow (along with France–Algeria and Italy–Egypt). Delay imposed at an upstream European centre reaches Moroccan airports as later arrivals, and departures from Morocco to Europe can receive calculated take-off times (CTOTs) when they are bound for regulated sectors.

## The rest of the picture

Network punctuality improved on a year earlier, according to the overview, with 73.5% of flights arriving and 69.4% departing within 15 minutes of schedule. Among states, all ten of the busiest saw fewer flights than in week 42, with Greece down the most (7%), a point also noted in an [IndexBox summary](https://www.indexbox.io/blog/european-air-traffic-falls-23-in-week-43-as-storms-austen-and-boelo-disrupt-flights/) of the report. Among airports, Madrid Barajas (+1%) was the only one of the ten busiest to grow on the previous week.

On the cost side, EUROCONTROL billed €1,135 million in en-route charges for September 2026, 3% more than in September 2025, and reports that European jet fuel averaged $4.84 a gallon in October, 114% above the pre-crisis average of January and February.
