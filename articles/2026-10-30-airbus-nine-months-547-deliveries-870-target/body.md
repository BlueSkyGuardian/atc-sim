Airbus delivered **547 commercial aircraft** in the first nine months of 2026, 40 more than a year earlier, and kept its target of **around 870 deliveries** for the full year, the manufacturer said on 28 October when it published its [nine-month results](https://www.airbus.com/en/newsroom/press-releases/2026-10-airbus-reports-nine-month-9m-2026-results). Reaching that figure means delivering about 323 aircraft in the last three months of the year, as [Aerospace Global News](https://aerospaceglobalnews.com/?p=53033) calculated after the September delivery count.

## What was delivered

The A320 Family still dominates: it accounts for **428** of the 547 aircraft delivered from January to September. Airbus also delivered **68 A220s**, **38 A350s** and **13 A330s**.

Orders were strong. Airbus booked 1,174 gross orders (1,105 net of cancellations), against 610 gross a year earlier, and its commercial backlog reached **9,310 aircraft** at the end of September, up from 8,665 a year before.

## Money and the third quarter

Revenues rose 7% to €50.6 billion over nine months, and the adjusted operating profit (EBIT Adjusted) rose 8% to €4.5 billion. The third quarter alone was weaker: revenues fell 2% and adjusted EBIT fell 9%, which Airbus attributes mainly to lower commercial deliveries and a less favourable mix. Free cash flow before customer financing was negative at -€1.27 billion, mostly because of inventory built up ahead of year-end deliveries.

Chief executive Guillaume Faury said the company was "focused on delivery while maintaining a steadfast focus on quality" in a complex geopolitical and operating environment. Airbus confirmed its 2026 guidance: around 870 deliveries, adjusted EBIT of about €7.5 billion and free cash flow before customer financing of about €4.5 billion, assuming no further disruption to trade, air traffic, the supply chain or operations.

## The fourth-quarter challenge

{{figure:target.svg}}

Aerospace Global News calculated that after 72 deliveries in September, Airbus had reached 62.9% of its target, with 323 aircraft still to deliver: roughly 108 a month in the fourth quarter, against an average of about 61 a month from January to September. In the fourth quarter of 2025, Airbus delivered 286 aircraft, including 136 in December; this year it needs 37 more than that. The same report noted that about 20 aircraft slipped from the third quarter into the fourth because of production delays, citing Reuters, that Pratt & Whitney engine shortages have constrained A320neo-family output, and that A330 deliveries were suspended in June and July after a tool was found in a tail section. Lars Wagner, head of Airbus Commercial Aircraft, told Reuters he was confident of meeting the target.

## Production rates

Airbus restated its production goals: 70 to 75 A320-family aircraft a month by the end of 2027, stabilising at 75 afterwards; 13 A220s a month in 2028; 12 A350s a month in 2028; and 5 A330s a month in 2029.

## What it means for air traffic

New aircraft do not by themselves add flights, but they set the pace at which airlines can grow or renew their fleets; Royal Air Maroc's plan to go from 50 to 200 aircraft by 2037 is one example close to home (see our separate article). For controllers, each delivery also changes the traffic mix, as newer types replace older ones with different climb and descent performance. The full-year delivery count, due at the start of next year, will show whether Airbus closes the gap.
