An electric air taxi landed at a major US airport on Friday after flying the route of the world's first scheduled airline. On 30 October, Joby Aviation flew its piloted electric vertical take-off and landing aircraft (eVTOL) from Albert Whitted Airport (SPG) in downtown St. Petersburg, Florida, across Tampa Bay to **Tampa International Airport (TPA)**, opening a week of flights in Florida under the US government's **eVTOL Integration Pilot Program (eIPP)**, according to [Joby's announcement](https://www.financialcontent.com/article/bizwire-2026-10-30-joby-brings-its-vertical-takeoff-and-landing-air-taxi-to-florida), [FOX 13](https://www.fox13news.com/news/electric-air-taxi-fly-from-st-pete-tampa-international-florida-test) and [DroneXL](https://dronexl.co/2026/10/30/joby-florida-eipp-tampa-bay-1914-airline-route/).

## A 1914 route, flown again

The route was chosen for its history. On 1 January 1914, Tony Jannus flew the former mayor of St. Petersburg, Abe Pheil, across Tampa Bay in what is recognised as the world's first scheduled commercial airline flight. The St. Petersburg-Tampa Airboat Line took about 23 minutes for the crossing, charged $5 one way and carried 1,205 passengers before its last flight on 5 May 1914, DroneXL recalls.

The aircraft that flew on Friday, registered **N545JX**, is a pre-production Joby air taxi flying under an experimental airworthiness certificate. Joby's design has six tilting propellers and is meant to carry a pilot and four passengers at about 200 mph (about 320 km/h), FOX 13 reports. Beta Technologies, another electric aircraft maker, also flew in Friday's demonstration between the two airports, according to a [report carried by Yahoo News](https://www.yahoo.com/news/us/articles/electric-aircraft-retrace-historic-tampa-213934320.html); "You witnessed history here today," Joby's President of Operations, Bonny Simi, told the spectators. None of the sources gave the duration of the flights.

## What the programme is for

The eIPP is a White House-backed programme announced by the US Department of Transportation in March 2026. The Florida Department of Transportation (FDOT) project is one of **eight selected** nationwide; it is planned in three phases covering passenger transport, cargo delivery, and automation and medical response. Texas held its own eIPP week with Joby in the Dallas-Fort Worth area in September.

The Florida week continues, weather permitting, with a flight to **Orlando International Airport (MCO)**, landing at a new vertiport near Terminal C and the train station, and a flight to the Shuttle Landing Facility at the **Kennedy Space Center**. For air traffic control, the Orlando leg is the most interesting: Joby says the routes and procedures there were developed over **two years of simulation** with the Federal Aviation Administration (FAA), NASA, the Greater Orlando Aviation Authority and **Orlando air traffic controllers**.

"Each flight provides crucial data and lessons learned that will help establish safety standards for everyday operations," said FAA Administrator Bryan Bedford, quoted in Joby's release. The campaign is meant to give the FAA and the state data on airport integration, noise and vertiport procedures.

## Certification: close, not yet

Joby says it is in the final stages of FAA type certification. The aircraft that flew in Florida is not the one being certified: Joby's first FAA-conforming aircraft, **N547JX**, first flew at Marina, California, on 11 March 2026 for type inspection authorization (TIA) testing, DroneXL notes. Joby had said FAA pilots were expected to begin flight testing "for credit" later in 2026; DroneXL could not confirm whether that has started. Joby's third-quarter results are due on 4 November.

Friday's flight is **not the start of commercial service**: no fare or booking date has been announced, FOX 13 and DroneXL both point out.

## Florida's plan

The flights are mapping the first phase of what FDOT calls its "Aerial Highway Network", a planned intercity air taxi system. In April, Governor Ron DeSantis signed House Bill 1093, allowing FDOT to fund vertiports statewide, and the department is building **SunTrax Air**, an advanced air mobility (AAM) test campus along Interstate 4, where testing is planned to begin by the end of 2026.

## Why it matters to controllers

Air taxis landing at busy airports such as Tampa and Orlando have to fit into the existing flows of airliners, with their own approach paths, procedures and phraseology. The eIPP flights are among the first real tests of that integration with working controllers, and the procedures developed with Orlando's controllers give an idea of the work that lies ahead for air traffic services wherever these aircraft are introduced.
