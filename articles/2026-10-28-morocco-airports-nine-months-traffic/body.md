Morocco's airports handled **29,330,764 passengers** between January and September 2026, 8.74% more than in the same period of 2025, according to figures from the Office National Des Aéroports (ONDA, now trading as Airports of Morocco) published on 26 and 27 October by [Aeronautique.ma](https://www.aeronautique.ma/Aeroports-marocains-le-trafic-progresse-de-874-a-fin-septembre-2026_a6128.html), [Le Guide du Maroc](https://leguide.ma/aeroports-du-maroc-plus-de-293-millions-de-passagers-a-fin-septembre/) and [Hespress](https://en.hespress.com/148003-moroccos-airports-handle-29-3-million-passengers-up-8-7-in-nine-months.html). For the people who work the frequencies, the more telling number is the one for aircraft: **214,607 movements**, up 9.70%, a little faster than passenger growth.

## Where the traffic goes

The ranking of airports has not changed. Casablanca Mohammed V (GMMN) handled 9,355,121 passengers in nine months, Marrakech-Ménara (GMMX) 8,113,680, Agadir-Al Massira (GMAD) 2,790,578, Tangier Ibn Battouta (GMTT) 2,327,329 and Rabat-Salé (GMME) 1,902,312. Casablanca and Marrakech together account for roughly six passengers in ten, as Aeronautique.ma notes.

September alone brought about 3.29 million passengers. ONDA's release gives 3,289,181; subtracting the January–August total published a month earlier (26,040,927, reported by [Morocco World News](https://www.moroccoworldnews.com/2026/10/343516/rabat-sale-airport-rolls-out-automated-self-bag-drop-in-national-deployment/)) from the nine-month total gives 3,289,837, a few hundred more; the sources do not explain the small gap.

International traffic dominates: 26,150,513 passengers (+8.73%), close to 89% of the total, against 3,180,251 domestic passengers (+8.88%). By market, Europe remains far ahead with 21,665,628 passengers (+9.05%). Africa grew 13.62% to 1,581,996, North America 13.23% to 771,932, and the Maghreb 8.75% to 396,080. South America showed the fastest relative growth (+33.48%) but from a small base of 81,185 passengers. The Middle East and Far East was the only market to fall, down 1.8% to 1,653,692.

## What it means in the tower and approach

Aircraft movements are what controllers actually count, and here the figures confirm a steady rise in workload. Of the 214,607 landings and take-offs recorded to the end of September, Mohammed V accounted for 73,194, Marrakech-Ménara for 53,164 and Agadir-Al Massira for 19,041, according to [Le Guide](https://leguide.ma/aeroports-du-maroc-plus-de-293-millions-de-passagers-a-fin-septembre/) and [Maroc Diplomatique](https://maroc-diplomatique.net/aeroports-du-maroc-plus-de-293-millions-de-passagers-a-fin-septembre-onda/).

The January–August figures put movements at 190,342. The difference between the two releases means that September added a little over 24,000 movements across the network, in a month that is no longer peak summer. Movements grew faster than passengers (9.70% against 8.74%), so the average number of passengers per movement fell slightly; ONDA's release does not break movements down by type of flight or between domestic and international traffic.

Air freight is rising faster still: 83,744 tonnes over nine months, up 14.29%.

## The background

The year is following the pattern of 2025, which Morocco World News describes as a record year with 36.3 million passengers. Growth has been remarkably even through 2026: the eight-month figures showed passengers up 8.81% and movements up 9.72%, almost the same rates as at the end of September.

This traffic arrives while the main airports are being rebuilt. Under its Airports 2030 plan, presented at the [Marrakech Airshow](https://www.aeronautique.ma/Marrakech-Airshow-2026-l-ONDA-met-en-scene-sa-transformation-sous-la-marque-Airports-of-Morocco_a6117.html) in early October, ONDA is preparing a new terminal, a new 3,500-metre runway and a new control tower at Casablanca Mohammed V, with capacity targets for Marrakech, Agadir, Tangier and Fès, ahead of the 2030 FIFA World Cup that Morocco will co-host. For controllers, more movements on airports that are also construction sites mean more temporary procedures, work-in-progress NOTAMs and changing taxi routes over the coming years.

## What comes next

The winter schedules now starting will show whether the growth holds outside the summer: Ryanair alone says it will offer 5.3 million seats to and from Morocco this winter (see our separate article). The October figures will tell whether the year is on course to beat the 36.3 million passengers of 2025.
