Morocco's civil aviation authority, the Direction Générale de l'Aviation Civile (DGAC), plans to grant **definitive aerodrome certification to four more airports in 2027: Tétouan, Al Hoceima, Dakhla and Essaouira**. The target appears in the report on the State services managed autonomously (SEGMA) that accompanies the 2027 Finance Bill, as reported on 29 October by [La Quotidienne](https://laquotidienne.ma/article/economie/aviation-civile-quatre-aeroports-vises-certification-definitive-2027) and [Anwar Press](https://anwarpress.com/741381/), which places the passage on pages 40 and 41 of the document.

## Where the airports stand

According to the same report, the DGAC's record for 2025 and the first half of 2026 includes:

- **definitive certification of Tangier Ibn Battouta** (GMTT);
- **renewal of the certificates of Marrakech-Menara (GMMX) and Agadir-Al Massira (GMAD)**;
- **provisional certification of Tétouan Sania Ramel (GMTN), Al Hoceima Cherif Al Idrissi (GMTA) and Dakhla (GMMH)**.

The fourth airport on the 2027 list, **Essaouira-Mogador (GMMI)**, is not mentioned among the provisional certificates in either article, and neither gives its current status. Anwar Press stresses that the 2027 objective is a programmed target, not a certificate already issued, and that certification should not be confused with the opening of a new airport or with an announcement of expansion works.

## What certification means

Aerodrome certification is the regulator's formal confirmation that an airport's infrastructure, equipment and operating procedures meet the applicable safety standards, those of the International Civil Aviation Organization (ICAO) Annex 14 in particular, and that the operator has a working safety management system. A provisional certificate allows operations to continue while remaining points are closed; the definitive certificate marks the end of that process.

For air traffic controllers at these airports the subject is concrete: the certified aerodrome manual describes the movement area, the runway and taxiway markings and lighting, the protection of obstacle surfaces and the coordination between the airport operator and the air traffic services unit. Three of the four airports on the 2027 list are smaller regional airports on the Mediterranean coast and in the south; Essaouira is gaining traffic, with Royal Air Maroc's new [Casablanca–Essaouira link starting on 1 November](https://www.aeronautique.ma/Royal-Air-Maroc-reliera-Casablanca-a-Essaouira-des-le-1er-novembre_a6120.html).

## Security and cybersecurity, including for air traffic services

The report also lists security work. La Quotidienne writes that the DGAC has finalised and validated the **sixth edition of the National Civil Aviation Security Programme**, which now includes cybersecurity provisions. Anwar Press adds that the actions carried out in 2025 and the first half of 2026 included **cybersecurity and security requirements for air traffic service providers**, alongside inspection programmes for aircraft and maintenance organisations.

## The rest of the 2027 programme

Among the other actions planned for 2027, the two articles list:

- an application to automate the granting and follow-up of **traffic rights** authorisations;
- the follow-up of partnership agreements with Royal Air Maroc aimed at reviving **domestic air transport**;
- training for safety inspectors in airworthiness (inspections, maintenance, continuing airworthiness management);
- the issue and renewal of approvals for **training organisations and flight simulation training devices**;
- safety and security inspections at airports;
- the preparation of the **second edition of the National Air Transport Facilitation Programme**, through workshops.

Neither article gives budget figures for these actions.

## Why it matters

Morocco's airports handled 29.3 million passengers in the first nine months of 2026, 8.74% more than a year earlier, according to ONDA figures carried by [Le360](https://ar.le360.ma/economie/IEIB757NINDJPNV3DQIBV7SNJM/). Bringing every airport with regular commercial traffic up to definitive certification is part of the safety foundation that this growth relies on, for airport operators, for the Office National des Aéroports (ONDA) and for the controllers who work there. The next milestone to watch is the DGAC's own announcement when each certificate is issued.
